# Teutons: culture brief (Holy Roman Empire and the Teutonic Order, c. 1200-1450)

Target look: **Brick Gothic** of the Teutonic Order's Prussia (Malbork/Marienburg, Toruń, Gdańsk), with red brick, white plaster panels and steep red-tile roofs. The army is white and black.

This differs from the current bible, which calls for "heavy grey stone, near-black slate". Recommendation: switch to red brick with dark-glazed brick patterns. It is more accurate and makes the Teutons instantly distinct from the grey Britons and the pale Franks. If dark roofs are kept for contrast, use dark glazed tile (`#4A2E2A`), not blue slate.

## 2. Architecture

- **Walls:** massive red brick on a granite or fieldstone plinth, with patterns of dark-glazed bricks.
- **Gables:** tall **stepped and pinnacled gables** with white-plastered blind arches and openwork tracery in brick.
- **Roofs:** steep, red tile. Towers are square (bergfried) with brick crenels or a tall pyramid or spire cap. There are timber hoardings and covered wall-walks, and wooden latrine towers (dansker) on arches.
- **Openings:** pointed brick arches with moulded brick jambs and small lancets.
- **Ornament:** the black cross on white over gates, and figures of the Virgin. Malbork has a large Madonna in a niche.
- **Props:** barrels of beer and grain sacks, a cart, a moat and a drawbridge.

**Buildings**
- **town_center:** a convent-castle (Ordensburg) high castle. It is a square four-winged red-brick block around a courtyard with a stepped gable, a tall square corner tower and a chapel with tracery windows.
- **house:** a half-timbered Fachwerk house with dark oak frame, white infill and a steep red tile gable facing the street. A brick variant is the Hanseatic town house with a stepped gable.
- **barracks:** a long brick dormitory hall with a red tile roof, small lancets and a gate arch with the cross.
- **archery_range:** a brick-walled yard with a timber shooting shed and crossbow targets.
- **stable:** a brick ground floor with a half-timbered hay loft and a red tile roof.
- **castle:** Malbork-style. A complex of brick wings with stepped gables, a tall square main tower, a gate tower with a drawbridge over a moat and timber hoardings on the walls.
- **watch_tower:** a square brick tower with crenels or a steep tile pyramid roof.
- **blacksmith:** a brick smithy with a big chimney and an open forge arch.
- **lumber_camp:** a half-timbered shed, a log pile and a sawhorse.
- **mining_camp:** a timber headframe with a treadwheel windlass. The Harz and Saxon silver mines (Agricola) are the reference.
- **mill:** a **watermill**, the dominant mill type of the Order's Prussia (Order mills on canals, as at Gdańsk). Draw a brick mill house with a big undershot wheel.
- **market:** a brick town hall in the style of Toruń's Ratusz, with arcaded stalls below.
- **stone_wall:** red brick wall on a fieldstone base with dark diaper bands and covered timber hoardings.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** wool tunic, braies and hose, and a **Gugel** (hood with a shoulder cape). Bearded. Brown, green and grey.
- **swordsman:** a city or imperial soldier. He wears a kettle hat (Eisenhut) or bascinet, a mail shirt under a coat of plates (cloth-covered), and a **Langes Messer** or arming sword with a heater shield. The surcoat is in black and gold.
- **spearman (halberd):** a kettle hat, a brigandine and a **halberd**, which is documented in German lands from the 14th century. He has a mail collar and a broad-brimmed helmet silhouette.
- **archer:** the Order and the German towns favoured the **crossbow**. That is the accurate choice if the game permits. With a bow: a light gambeson, a hood and a grey tunic with a black tau cross. Half-brother and sergeant colouring is the defensible option here.
- **teutonic_knight (on foot):** the defining unit.
  - **Head:** a **great helm (Topfhelm)**, flat-topped or sugar-loaf, with no visible face.
  - **Body:** a full mail hauberk, and from the 14th century plate limbs and a coat of plates.
  - **Surcoat:** a **white** surcoat or mantle with a **black cross**.
  - **Shield and weapon:** a white heater shield with a black cross and a longsword or war hammer.
  - **At a glance:** pure white and black, the bulkiest silhouette.
- **cavalry:** a mounted knight brother in a great helm with a white surcoat, black cross and lance. As a variant, an imperial knight with a gold surcoat and black eagle.
  - **Horse:** a heavy warhorse with a white caparison bearing a black cross and a high saddle.

## 4. Heraldry

- **Teutonic Order:** a **black cross on white**. Knight brothers wore white mantles; serving brothers and sergeants wore **grey** with a **black tau (T) cross**, though sources differ on the details. For a 5-9 px emblem use a plain black cross on white.
- **Holy Roman Empire:** a **single-headed** black eagle on gold. The double-headed eagle dates only from Sigismund (1433), so use it only for a late look.

## 5. Avoid

- The Iron Cross, the Balkenkreuz and any modern German military insignia.
- The double-headed eagle before 1433.
- Landsknecht puffs-and-slashes and Zweihänder swords, which belong to 1490s-1530s mercenaries.
- Horns or antlers on the Order's great helms. These come from film and lean on Alexander Nevsky.
- Grey stone Rhine castles mixed with Brick Gothic in the same building.
