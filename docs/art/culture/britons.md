# Britons: culture brief (medieval England and Wales, c. 1070-1450)

Target look: Norman and Edwardian England. The buildings are squat, massive grey stone keeps and round-towered castles, with thatch and half-timber villages. The units mix St George crosses, Plantagenet red and gold, and the **longbow**. The Britons must read as heavier, lower and greyer than the Franks.

## 2. Architecture

- **Walls:** coursed rubble with ashlar quoins. Norman work has flat pilaster buttresses and round-headed arches. Village buildings are cruck-framed or post-and-truss timber with wattle-and-daub.
- **Roofs:** castles and keeps have flat leaded roofs hidden behind crenels, with slate only on towers and chapels. Cottages have thick thatch with rounded eaves.
- **Openings:** round-headed (Romanesque) doors and small paired windows on keeps, pointed arches only after about 1200. Keep entrances sit on the first floor, reached by a forebuilding stair.
- **Ornament:** chevron (zig-zag) moulding on Norman arches, corner turrets and crenellations.
- **Props:** archery butts, hay ricks, wattle fences, a well and a sheaf of arrows in a barrel.

**Buildings**
- **town_center:** a Norman great tower like the White Tower or Rochester. It is a tall rectangular stone keep with four corner turrets, pilaster buttresses and a forebuilding stair, with a slate-roofed hall beside it.
- **house:** a cruck-framed cottage. Limewashed daub walls, a thick thatch roof and a wattle fence.
- **barracks:** a stone-footed timber hall with a slate roof, a weapon rack and a St George banner.
- **archery_range:** a meadow with turf-mound butts and round straw targets, a bow rack and a timber shelter. Archery practice was mandated by law.
- **stable:** a timber-framed stable with thatch and a stone trough.
- **castle:** an Edwardian concentric castle in the manner of Harlech or Beaumaris. Inner and outer walls, round drum towers and a massive twin-towered gatehouse.
- **watch_tower:** a square stone tower with crenels and a wooden hoarding top.
- **blacksmith:** a stone smithy with a thatched roof, an open forge with a hood, and horseshoes on the wall.
- **lumber_camp:** a sawpit with trestles, oak logs and a thatched lean-to.
- **mining_camp:** a timber windlass over a shaft, ore barrows. Cornish tin and Welsh lead are good references.
- **mill:** a **post windmill** with an open trestle and four common sails. These are documented in England from the 1180s.
- **market:** a market cross (stone shaft with steps) with canvas-topped stalls.
- **stone_wall:** grey rubble wall with crenels and arrow-loops.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** knee-length wool tunic in russet or green, hose and a hood with a short liripipe (chaperon). Bearded or clean-shaven.
- **swordsman:** a foot man-at-arms. He wears a mail hauberk under a white surcoat with a red St George cross and a bascinet with aventail, and carries an arming sword and a heater shield.
- **spearman:** a quilted jack, a kettle hat, a spear and a small round buckler or no shield. The English **bill** is the more characteristic late-medieval polearm if a variant is allowed.
- **archer:** a Welsh or levy bowman. Bare-headed or hooded, no armour, a hip-height self-bow and a short tunic. He is smaller and lighter than the longbowman.
- **longbowman:** the defining unit. His bow is **as tall as he is or taller** (about 1.8-2 m, yew), drawn to the ear, with arrows tucked in his belt. He wears a padded jack and a kettle hat or open sallet, and carries a sword and buckler at the hip. Body colour is a white-and-red St George jack, or green and russet. Agincourt-era archers were often lightly armoured; brigandines are mainly later 15th century.
- **cavalry:** a mounted knight in a great helm or bascinet, mail and plate, with a red surcoat bearing three gold lions and a lance.
  - **Horse:** a solid bay or grey with a red caparison bearing gold lions, a high saddle and a breastband.


**People (physique).** Stature a little above the shared base (+2 %), average build. Skin: fair, ruddy and light tones, spread over the roles. Hair brown, red or blond; beards short or full, clean-shaven also common. Tones, hair colours and the roles that get each are set in `art_source/blender/units/unitstyle.py` (PHYSIQUE) and mirrored in `art_source/aseprite/physique.lua`. Facial features (eyes, nose, cheekbones) are deliberately the same for every civ: varying them reads as caricature in this style.

## 4. Heraldry

- **Royal arms:** *Gules, three lions passant guardant Or*. They first appear on Richard I's second great seal, made in 1195 and in use from 1198. At 5-9 px use **three tiny gold horizontal lion-stripes on red**, or one lion.
- **St George's cross:** a red cross on white, the soldiers' badge from the late 13th century. This is the best tiny shield emblem.
- **Welsh variant:** the four lions of Gwynedd, quartered red and gold with counterchanged lions.
- **Welsh dragon:** the red dragon flag is Tudor (1485) and later, so skip it.

## 5. Avoid

- Feathered Robin Hood caps and short longbows.
- The Union Jack, kilts and tartan (Scottish, and later).
- Tudor ruffs and Gothic French spires.
