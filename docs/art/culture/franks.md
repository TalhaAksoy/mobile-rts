# Franks: culture brief (Capetian France, c. 1100-1400)

Target look: Île-de-France Gothic. The buildings are tall pale limestone with steep blue-grey slate, pointed arches and spires. The units are mail and surcoats in royal azure and gold. Height and verticality are what tell the Franks apart from the squat Briton keep.

## 2. Architecture

- **Walls:** smooth ashlar limestone in regular courses, flying buttresses and pinnacles on big buildings, and town houses in timber-frame over a stone ground floor.
- **Roofs:** very steep (55-60°) slate, conical "pepper-pot" slate caps on round towers, lead ridge crests and gilt finials.
- **Openings:** pointed lancets, rose windows, gabled portals with tympanum, and arrow-loops on military work.
- **Ornament:** crockets, gargoyles, fleur-de-lis finials and pennons on the cones.
- **Props:** wine barrels, carts, well with a slate hood and banners on the towers.

**Buildings**
- **town_center:** a royal palace on the model of the Palais de la Cité. It is a long stone great hall with a steep slate roof, attached to a tall Gothic chapel (Sainte-Chapelle) with a lead spire and rose window.
- **house:** a narrow two-storey timber-framed town house with white plaster infill, a jettied upper floor and a steep slate or tile gable to the street.
- **barracks:** a stone hall with buttresses and a slate roof, a weapon rack and a fleur-de-lis banner.
- **archery_range:** a timber shed with a slate pentice, butts of straw targets and a crossbow rack.
- **stable:** a long half-timbered stable with a low stone plinth, hay loft door and a horse trough.
- **castle:** Philip Augustus style. A curtain wall with round towers under conical slate caps, a twin-tower gate, and a cylindrical donjon like the Louvre "Grosse Tour" or Coucy.
- **watch_tower:** a slim round stone tower with a slate pepper-pot cone and a pennon.
- **blacksmith:** a stone ground floor with an open forge arch and a brick chimney, an anvil and a quench tub outside.
- **lumber_camp:** an open timber shed with a sawpit and stacked oak.
- **mining_camp:** a timber headframe with a windlass, carts and stone rubble.
- **mill:** a post windmill (in northern France since the late 12th century) on a timber trestle with four lattice sails.
- **market:** a timber market hall (halle) on posts under a huge slate roof.
- **stone_wall:** pale ashlar with square crenels and a projecting wall-walk.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** wool cotte to the knee, hose, belted. Linen coif or straw hat, beardless or short beard, undyed and russet colours.
- **swordsman:** knee-length mail hauberk with an azure surcoat, a kettle hat (chapel de fer) or nasal helm, a heater shield in azure with a gold fleur-de-lis, and an arming sword. The surcoat reads as a long coloured tunic, which marks him out.
- **spearman:** a padded gambeson (aketon) in off-white or russet, kettle hat, and a 2.5 m spear. He has a small heater shield or none. He is a lighter, paler shape than the swordsman.
- **archer:** historically France relied on crossbowmen, often Genoese mercenaries. A **crossbow** is the defensible choice. If the game requires a bow, use a plain self-bow. He wears a gambeson, a mail coif or open bascinet and a quiver at the hip.
- **knight (mounted):** a cylinder great helm with a crest or fan, a mail hauberk with mail chausses, an azure surcoat semé with gold lis and a heater shield to match. He carries a long lance with a pennon.
  - **Horse:** a tall, heavy destrier with a full-length azure caparison semé with lis, a high-cantled war saddle and a red bridle.


**People (physique).** Stature +1 %, average build. Skin: light, fair and ruddy tones. Hair mostly brown, some blond, worn in the Norman / Capetian **bowl cut** (a straight fringe); short beards or clean-shaven. Tones, hair colours and the roles that get each are set in `art_source/blender/units/unitstyle.py` (PHYSIQUE) and mirrored in `art_source/aseprite/physique.lua`. Facial features (eyes, nose, cheekbones) are deliberately the same for every civ: varying them reads as caricature in this style.

## 4. Heraldry

- **Arms:** *Azure semé de lis Or*, the field strewn with gold fleurs-de-lis. These are royal from about 1180-1200 (Philip Augustus's seal shows the lis by 1180). At 5-9 px use **one gold lis on blue**.
- **Later arms:** "France Modern", three lis, dates from 1376 and is acceptable only for a late look.
- **Banner:** the Oriflamme, a red forked war banner of Saint-Denis, is a good flag variant. Dating of the first royal adoption varies between Louis VII and Philip II; the brief follows c. 1180.

## 5. Avoid

- Full plate armour on 12th-13th-century knights. Plate arrives after 1350.
- The tricolour, the Napoleonic eagle and Renaissance châteaux (Chambord-style dormers).
- Musketeer hats.
- A Bourbon white flag with lis. That one is 17th century and later.
