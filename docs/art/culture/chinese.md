# Chinese (Song/Yuan/early Ming) — art brief

Target period: c. 1000–1450. Song architecture (*Yingzao Fashi*, 1103) and early Ming fortifications.

## 2. Architecture

**Walls:** a timber frame of lacquered red columns on a raised stone/rammed-earth platform, with plastered white or wooden lattice infill between columns. **Roofs:** grey tile in long half-cylinder rows; **hip-and-gable** (*xieshan*) on halls, gable on houses. **Eaves curve gently upward** at the corners (Song curves are moderate, not the extreme Qing flick). Use deep overhangs on visible **dougong** bracket clusters painted blue-green. **Ridge ornaments:** a *chiwen* (fish/owl-tail) ornament at each ridge end; small ridge figures. **Gates:** a gatehouse pavilion (*chenglou*) on a brick-faced platform; trapezoid timber-framed gate openings are Song, round-arched tunnels Yuan/Ming. **Windows/doors:** lattice doors filling the full bay, round moon windows, lattice windows. **Towers:** multi-storey brick or timber pagodas. **Props:** stone lions, lanterns, tall flagpoles, bamboo, willow, water jars, wheelbarrows, rice-straw stacks.

- **town_center:** a yamen/palace hall: a grand hip-and-gable hall on a tall stepped white stone platform, red columns, painted brackets, a forecourt gate.
- **house:** a single-storey courtyard house: white walls, grey tile gable roof, wooden lattice door, low courtyard wall.
- **barracks:** a long timber hall with a grey tile roof, weapon racks of halberds, red banners, a drum stand.
- **archery_range:** a walled yard with round straw targets painted with concentric rings, a covered viewing pavilion.
- **stable:** an open-sided timber shed with a tile roof, troughs, a stone hitching post.
- **castle:** a walled city gate: thick brick-faced rammed-earth wall, a large multi-storey *chenglou* gate tower on top, an outer barbican (*wengcheng*), crenellations.
- **watch_tower:** a square brick tower with a small tiled pavilion on top; **beacon smoke** for alarms.
- **blacksmith:** a brick workshop with an open front, a box bellows (double-acting piston bellows) and a brick forge; crossbow parts on racks.
- **lumber_camp:** a bamboo-and-timber shed, log stacks, a two-man frame saw.
- **mining_camp:** a timber adit, baskets on carrying poles, an ore crusher.
- **mill:** a **watermill**: a vertical wooden wheel on a tile-roofed mill house over a stream, with a trip-hammer (Song water-powered trip-hammers and grain mills are well documented; windmills in China were rare and late).
- **market:** a two-storey shop row with banner signs (vertical cloth signboards), awnings, as in *Along the River During Qingming*.
- **stone_wall:** grey brick facing over rammed earth, rectangular crenellations with arrow slits.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** hair in a **topknot covered by a dark cloth headwrap**, or a conical bamboo/straw hat for field work; hemp short jacket crossing left over right, trousers, cloth shoes or straw sandals; beard optional.
- **swordsman:** iron helmet with a flared lamellar neck guard; **lamellar armour with a long lamellar skirt** to the knee, shoulder guards; red coat underneath; **single-edged *dao*** sabre; a **tall rectangular/pentagonal wooden infantry shield** painted with a tiger or demon face (Wujing Zongyao shield). Reads as: big painted shield + saber.
- **spearman:** same helmet, lamellar to the waist only; long **qiang** spear with a red tassel below the blade, or a *ji* halberd; no shield. Reads as: tassel spear.
- **archer:** a soft red/black headwrap (*futou* or bandanna), padded red coat, no metal armour; Chinese recurve bow with a pronounced ear, quiver at the right hip. Reads as: no armour, bow.
- **chu_ko_nu (repeating crossbow):** a light padded or paper-armour vest, a cloth hat; a **crossbow with a tall box magazine on top and a rear lever**, worked with both hands; a bolt bag. Reads as: crossbow + box, lever up. *The weapon's Song service is debated; Wujing Zongyao and Ming Wubei Zhi describe it. Treat it as a militia/defence weapon.*
- **cavalry:** iron helmet with a red plume/tassel, full lamellar with arm guards and a round chest-mirror plate (*huxinjing*); **lance with a pennon** or a dao, bow case. **Horse:** a medium-sized Mongolian/North China type, sturdy; a saddle with a padded cloth, red tassels hanging under the neck and at the breast strap; optional **lamellar barding** for heavy cavalry, red or black lacquered.

## 4. Heraldry and emblems

- **Flags:** rectangular or triangular banners with a **single large surname or dynasty character** (宋 Song, 明 Ming) in black on red, or on white. A serrated "flame" border is common in Song/Ming paintings.
- **Shield devices:** tiger faces, demon masks (*taotie*-like) on infantry shields.
- **Do not use the dragon.** The imperial dragon was reserved for the emperor's own robes and flags, so use a dragon only on the town_center flag, if anywhere.

## 5. Common mistakes

- Qing queues and shaved foreheads (1644+); Qing yellow-glazed roofs everywhere.
- Extreme upturned "pagoda" eaves; Japanese samurai armour or torii gates.
- Peasant conical hats on soldiers; Western kite shields.
- Using the Great Wall in its Ming brick form for Song-era walls (Song walls were mainly rammed earth).
