# Vikings: culture brief (Norse Scandinavia, c. 800-1100)

Target look: low, long timber halls with curved walls and huge roofs of shingle, thatch or turf, carved animal ornament, earth-and-timber ring forts, and round painted shields. Everything is wood, turf and iron. **No stone castles.**

## 2. Architecture

- **Walls:** vertical planks or stave-built timber walls, or turf walls on a stone footing (Iceland and Borg). Walls are **bow-sided**, wider in the middle, like a boat. Inclined outer posts brace them on the Trelleborg type.
- **Roofs:** one massive hipped or gabled roof almost to the ground, covered in wooden shingles, thatch or turf, with a smoke hole and no chimney.
- **Openings:** few or no windows and low plank doors with iron strap hinges.
- **Ornament:** carved dragon or serpent gable-ends and interlace in **Borre, Mammen, Ringerike and Urnes** styles. Dragon-head gables are best attested on post-Viking stave churches (12th century), so use them as a stylised flourish.
- **Props:** shield rows on walls, rune stones painted red and black, drying fish racks, upturned boats, an anvil stump and a loom.

**Buildings**
- **town_center:** a chieftain's **great hall** (Borg or Lejre). A very long bow-sided longhouse (Borg was about 83 m) with a dark shingle roof, carved gable posts and a shield-lined door.
- **house:** a small turf-roofed pit house or short longhouse with grass on the roof and plank walls.
- **barracks:** a Trelleborg-style hall with plank walls, sloping outer posts and a shingle roof, with shields on the walls.
- **archery_range:** a cleared yard with straw and hide targets on posts and a lean-to.
- **stable:** a low turf-roofed byre with wattle partitions.
- **castle:** a **Trelleborg ring fortress**. A circular earth rampart with timber facing and a palisade, four gates at the cardinal points and longhouses inside.
- **watch_tower:** a simple timber platform tower with a ladder and a horn. Fortified towers are poorly attested, so this is a game convenience.
- **blacksmith:** an open-sided turf shed, a clay forge with bellows and an anvil on a log.
- **lumber_camp:** pine logs, an adze bench and a half-built boat strake.
- **mining_camp:** bog-iron pit with a clay shaft furnace for smelting and slag heaps.
- **mill:** a **small horizontal watermill** ("Norse mill"), a tiny plank hut over a stream. Hand querns were most common. No windmills.
- **market:** a Hedeby or Birka style trading place. Plank boardwalks, open stalls, scales and a boat moored alongside.
- **stone_wall:** an earth and turf rampart faced with timber and topped by a palisade of sharpened stakes.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** a wool tunic to mid-thigh, trousers with wrap leggings and a wool cap or hood. Beards and moustaches are common, and long or tied hair is fine. Undyed colours with a coloured hem.
- **swordsman:** a high-status warrior. He wears a rounded iron helmet with a **spectacle guard** (Gjermundbu) and a **mail byrnie**, and carries a sword. His **round shield** (about 80-90 cm) is painted with an iron boss.
- **spearman:** a wool tunic, perhaps a padded coat, a conical helmet or a cap, a spear and a round shield. Spear plus shield is the most common Viking warrior silhouette. Give him more cloth and less iron than the swordsman.
- **archer:** a tall yew or elm self-bow (the Hedeby bow, about 1.9 m). No armour, a hood and a quiver on the back or hip.
- **berserker:** a **wolf or bear pelt** (úlfheðnar, "wolf-coats") over the head and shoulders, bare arms, a **long-hafted Dane axe** (two-handed, late 10th-11th century) or sword and shield. No helmet, wild hair. Whether berserkers fought bare-chested is debated, so use a pelt over a tunic.
- **cavalry:** a game invention, since Vikings rode to battle and fought on foot. Use a mounted warrior with a spear, round shield and helmet.
  - **Horse:** a small, sturdy Nordic horse (Icelandic- or Fjord-type with a dun coat and dark mane). Simple saddle and bridle, no barding, a woven saddle blanket in red or blue.

## 4. Heraldry

There was no formal heraldry before about 1150.

- **Shields:** plain colours, halves, quarters, pinwheels and rings in yellow, black, red, white or blue. The Gokstad ship carried 64 shields painted yellow or black.
- **Raven banner:** attested in sagas and the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle. It was a triangular or quarter-round flag with a raven, used as the civ banner. At 5-9 px use a black raven silhouette on white or yellow.
- **Variants:** a black raven on yellow, or a black-yellow split shield.

## 5. Avoid

- **Horned or winged helmets**, a 19th-century opera invention.
- Studded leather armour, double-bladed fantasy axes and fur on everyone.
- Celtic knots in place of Norse animal styles, and runes as random letters.
- Stone castles or crenellations, and dragon ships on land.
- Modern undercut hairstyles, and mail on every unit, since mail was rare and costly.
