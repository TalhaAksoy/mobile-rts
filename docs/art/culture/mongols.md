# Mongols — art brief

Target period: c. 1200–1370 (Chinggis Khan to the Yuan/Ilkhanate). A nomadic culture: buildings are mostly felt and timber.

## 2. Architecture

**Walls:** the *ger* is the core form: a circular lattice wall (*khana*) covered in white felt, bound by three horizontal rope bands, with a low conical roof and a smoke ring (*toono*) on top. Doors are low, wooden and painted orange-red with simple knot or spiral patterns. **Big structures:** large **ger-carts** (a ger on an ox-drawn platform cart) for khans; rows of carts; a few Chinese-built timber halls in Karakorum with grey tiled roofs. **Fortification:** rammed-earth ramparts and timber palisades, not stone castles. **Ornament:** felt appliqué in spiral/horn patterns, horsehair *tug* standards, felt rugs, wooden saddle racks. **Props:** wooden carts with big spoked wheels, hitching lines (*tsagdag*), milk churns, drying meat racks, horses, camels and sheep everywhere.

- **town_center:** the khan's palace-ger (*ordo*): an oversized golden-white ger on a large ox-cart platform or low wooden base, with smaller gers around it and tug banners at the door.
- **house:** a single small ger with an orange door; a cart and a churn beside it.
- **barracks:** a cluster of 3 gers inside a wicker or timber fence with a weapons rack and a black tug banner.
- **archery_range:** open steppe with a felt target on a pole, a line of leather target cylinders (period Mongol archery targets), and a shade awning.
- **stable:** a horse hitching line (rope between two posts), saddles on wooden racks, a felt windbreak; no closed barn.
- **castle:** rammed-earth wall enclosure (Karakorum-style) with timber gate towers and grey-tiled Chinese-style roofs on the gate, a large ger visible inside. Mongol engineers were often Chinese or Persian, so this is defensible.
- **watch_tower:** a tall timber lookout frame with a felt-covered platform and a horsetail tug on top.
- **blacksmith:** an open felt-roofed shelter with a portable forge, skin bellows, anvil on a stump, arrowheads.
- **lumber_camp:** a cart loaded with lattice poles and logs, a small felt shelter.
- **mining_camp:** a cart, ore baskets, a felt shelter by a rock outcrop.
- **mill:** no native windmills or watermills. Use a **storage ger with a stone hand-quern** and sacks of grain on a cart. Optionally add a small Yuan-period horizontal watermill. The quern is more defensible.
- **market:** a caravan stop: camels, carts, an awning of felt and silk, bolts of cloth, Muslim and Chinese traders (Karakorum had both quarters).
- **stone_wall:** rammed earth with a timber parapet.

## 3. Units

- **villager:** fur-trimmed felt hat with an upturned brim or a pointed cap with ear flaps; **hair: crown shaved, side locks braided into loops behind the ears, small forelock**; thin moustache; brown or blue wool **deel** (long robe closing at the right side, coloured sash), soft boots with upturned toes. Women: deel, braids.
- **swordsman:** iron bowl helmet with a lamellar/leather neck guard; **lamellar** of lacquered leather or iron, knee-length, with shoulder plates; a **small round wicker shield**; a slightly curved sabre. Reads as: shield + sabre on foot.
- **spearman:** padded deel or leather lamellar, helmet with a plume tube, **spear with a hook under the blade**, no shield or a wicker one. Reads as: hooked spear.
- **archer:** fur hat, unarmoured deel with sash, Mongol composite bow (long stiff ears, string bridges), quiver on the right hip **and** bow case on the left. Reads as: hat + bow, no armour.
- **horse_archer:** helmet (tall conical bowl with a tube plume of red horsehair, leather flaps) or fur hat; a light lamellar or padded coat; bow drawn on horseback, two quivers, a sabre. **Horse:** the **Mongolian pony**: short (~13 hands), stocky, big head, thick neck, shaggy mane, dun/bay/grey; high wooden saddle with raised front and rear arches, very short stirrups (rider stands high), no barding. Reads as: small horse + bow.
- Heavy cavalry (optional): full lamellar with lamellar horse barding in five pieces (Carpini describes it).


**People (physique).** A little shorter (-2 %) and stocky (+6 % torso), the build of lifelong riders. Skin: honey, weathered tan and warm tones. Hair black: **crown shaved, a small forelock, the side locks braided into loops behind the ears** (Carpini, Rubruck); thin moustache. Tones, hair colours and the roles that get each are set in `art_source/blender/units/unitstyle.py` (PHYSIQUE) and mirrored in `art_source/aseprite/physique.lua`. Facial features (eyes, nose, cheekbones) are deliberately the same for every civ: varying them reads as caricature in this style.

## 4. Heraldry and emblems

- **Tug/sulde:** a spear-tipped pole with a ring of horsehair below the blade. The **black tug was the war banner**, the **white tug (nine-tailed) the peace/enthronement banner** (*Secret History* style accounts). For 5–9 px: a black horsetail standard, or a white tug.
- On shields: plain colours or simple spirals. There was **no Mongol heraldry**.
- The Soyombo symbol is 17th century, so do not use it.

## 5. Common mistakes

- Horned or Viking helmets; Manchu queue hairstyle (Qing).
- Fur loincloth "barbarian" look. Mongols wore tailored silk and wool coats.
- Stone castles, or big horses (they rode small ponies).
- Modern Mongolian flag/Soyombo; overuse of wolf-pelt hats.
- Heavy plate armour.
